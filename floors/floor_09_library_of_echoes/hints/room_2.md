`FlatIndex` is one (N, D) array of normalised vectors plus a list of ids; `search` is a single matrix-vector product followed by room 1's `top_k`, and `comparisons += N`. For `KMeans`, squared distances between (N, D) points and (K, D) centroids come from `|x|² + |c|² − 2 x·c`, no (N, K, D) tensor needed; `assign` is the argmin over K. The update is the mean of each cluster's points; a cluster with no points keeps its old centroid.
---
k-means++: the first centre is a random row; then repeat: `d2 = min over chosen centres of squared distance`, and pick the next centre with `rng.choice(n, p=d2 / d2.sum())`. Record inertia as `d2[np.arange(N), labels].sum()` right after each assignment, before moving the centroids. `IVFIndex` keeps one list of vectors and one of ids per cluster. `search`: `probe = np.argsort(dist_to_centroids)[:nprobe]`, stack the probed clusters' vectors, score with a dot product, `top_k`, and add the number of candidates scored to `comparisons`.
---
`recall_at_k`: for each pair of result lists take the first k, turn each item into an id (`item[0] if isinstance(item, tuple) else item`), and compute `len(set_a & set_b) / k`; return `float(np.mean(...))`. If your IVF recall is low but `nprobe == n_clusters` matches flat exactly, check that `search` ranks centroids with the SAME distance `assign` uses; if your comparisons are too high, you are scoring every vector and filtering afterwards.
