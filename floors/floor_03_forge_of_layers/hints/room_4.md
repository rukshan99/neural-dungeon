`early_stopping`: `best = int(np.argmin(val_losses))`; stop when `len(val_losses) - 1 - best >= patience`. `evaluate_loss` is Room 3's `accuracy` with `loss.forward(model.forward(X), y)` in the middle and the same eval/restore dance around it. `diagnose` is the three-step rule from the docstring, written as three `if`s in order; `np.argmin(val)` gives you the epoch to compare train against.
---
`train_with_validation` without patience is a loop of `epochs` iterations that does `train_hist.extend(train(model, loss, opt, X_train, y_train, 1, batch_size, rng))` then `val_hist.append(evaluate_loss(model, loss, X_val, y_val))`. Calling Room 3's `train` for one epoch at a time guarantees the training curve matches `train()` exactly, which the trial checks.
---
With patience: after appending the val loss, `best, stop = early_stopping(val_hist, patience)`; if `best == len(val_hist) - 1` this epoch is a new low, so `best_state = {k: v.copy() for k, v in model.params().items()}`; if `stop`, `break`. After the loop, `for k, p in model.params().items(): p[...] = best_state[k]` copies the best parameters back into the model's own arrays. For the prophecy: the apprentice's val goes 0.60 -> 0.88 while train hits 0.03 (overfitting); the hammer's train never leaves 0.97 (underfitting); the blade's val settles at its minimum (healthy).
