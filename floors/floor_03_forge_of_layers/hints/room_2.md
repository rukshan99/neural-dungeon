Each initializer is one line: a draw from `rng` of shape `(fan_in, fan_out)` times a scale. `rng.uniform(-a, a, size=...)` for Xavier with `a = sqrt(6 / (fan_in + fan_out))`; `rng.standard_normal((fan_in, fan_out)) * std` for the normals with `std = sqrt(2 / fan_in)` (He) or the fixed constant. For the prophecy, compute the per-layer variance factor `fan_in * Var(w)`: 256 * 0.01^2 = 0.0256 for small_normal; 256 * 2/512 = 1 for Xavier; 256 * 2/256 = 2 for He (ReLU then halves it); 256 * 1 = 256 for large_normal.
---
`activation_statistics`: `x = rng.standard_normal((n_samples, width))`, then loop `depth` times: `W = init_fn(width, width, rng)`, `x = act(x @ W)`, record `x.std()` and `np.mean(np.abs(x) > 0.99)`. Draw `x` first and one `W` per layer, in that order, so the trial's reference walk sees the same random numbers. `act` is `np.tanh` or `lambda z: np.maximum(z, 0)`.
---
The fates: a per-layer factor below 1 compounds to nothing over 20 layers (`0.16**20` for small_normal+tanh; `sqrt(1/2)**20 = 1e-3` for Xavier+ReLU, because ReLU discards half the variance and Xavier did not pay for it) -> "collapses". Xavier+tanh and He+ReLU hold their scale -> "healthy". large_normal makes pre-activations of std ~16: tanh pins nearly every unit at +-1 (std ~1 but ~87% saturated), ReLU has no ceiling and the std multiplies by ~11 per layer -> both "saturates".
